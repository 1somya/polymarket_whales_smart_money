Part 3

Adding the losers back

Everything so far used the top-500 wallets by PnL — winners only. You can't tell skill from luck without losers, and you can't build a baseline without the full distribution. Part 3 builds a profitability-neutral cohort of thousands of wallets, sampled straight from the fill log.

01 · THE BLOCKING PROBLEM

Survivorship bias

The lum.id leaderboard is sorted by PnL and capped at 500 — so our cohort was the winners. Every claim about "what distinguishes good wallets" is meaningless without the wallets that failed: the top decile isn't impressive if the whole field did just as well.

Why it's hard: lum.id can't hand you losers (it only ranks winners). So we need a way to enumerate wallets that is neutral with respect to profitability — one that samples on activity, not on winning.

02 · THE METHOD

Time-slice fill sampling

The order-book subgraph holds every fill ever. If we sample fills scattered across the whole history and collect the distinct wallet addresses, we get a sample that conditions on trading, not on winning — so it naturally includes losers.

Why it's PnL-neutral: a fill happens whether the wallet made or lost money. Sampling by fill-occurrence has no idea who won. It is activity-weighted (frequent traders appear more often) — a bias we measure and report, but it's orthogonal to profitability, which is the property we needed to de-bias.

03 · RECONSTRUCTION

From candidates to a cohort

Reconstructing every candidate is expensive, so we calibrated first: sampling across appearance tiers (how often a wallet showed up) told us where the followable wallets are and how much each tier costs to fetch.

Activity tier≥30-resolved hit-rateavg fillscharacter

The lesson: the hyperactive tier (≥5 appearances) is mostly market-makers — huge to fetch (median 6,635 fills, often hitting the cap) and exactly the non-followable population. The low tier still qualifies ~47% of the time and holds the moderate directional holders we care about. So we capped fills at 8,000 per wallet (moderate wallets fit under it; hyperactive ones truncate, flagged) and fetched in parallel.

07 · WHAT BECAME POSSIBLE

Claims unlocked

  1. Baselines for persistence. Random / median / bottom-decile wallets now exist to test the top decile against — without them, "the top decile persisted" is uninterpretable.
  2. Winner-vs-loser discrimination. Losers are in-sample, so "what separates them" is answerable.
  3. Rank-within-the-field. Forward rank tests can judge the top decile against the real distribution, not just against other survivors.
