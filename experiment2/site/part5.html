Part 5 · interpretation

Is any wallet worth following?

Yes — but the edge is small, lumpy, fades gradually over at least half a year without fully disappearing, and cuts both ways (avoiding bad wallets and picking good ones are comparably useful). Genuine steady compounders are ~1% of the field. Here is the whole study, answered honestly.

THE QUESTION

What this study was, and wasn't

This was the feasibility gate for a copy-trading rule. The full rule has two halves: (a) is a wallet worth following at all, and (b) is a given trade worth copying. This study covered (a) only. If no wallet's edge survived out-of-sample, there would be no "when to follow" — the answer would just be never. It doesn't.

Q1

Is the holder/trader split two types, or a spectrum?

A spectrum. The dip test finds trading_share unimodal (p ≈ 1.0) on both the winners-only and the full 2,055-wallet cohort. There is no clean "traders vs holders" dichotomy — wallets sit on one continuum, overwhelmingly on the holding end.

Q2

How much of this market is followable?

98.2% of cleanly-measured profit is holding-based — a copyable outcome edge, not execution. caveat measured on the fill-reconstructable, non-CTF set: 34.0% of volume and 19.6% of resolved PnL sit outside it (market-makers via CTF minting), so the true market-wide trader share is higher — we just can't measure it cleanly.

Q3

Does any ranking metric beat random, out-of-sample?

Yes — most metrics do, forecasting skill most reliably. The test: for each wallet, good_past = its historical metric was positive, good_fwd = its forward ROI (in a disjoint later window) was positive. We report P(good_fwd｜good_past) vs. P(good_fwd｜bad_past) vs. the unconditional baseline.

Conviction-sizing barely beats baseline on returns — though the sizing behavior itself is a stable trait (sizing_gap → forward sizing_gap: 67% vs. 53%, sig. in 6/9 windows). The signal moves roughly symmetrically in both directions: avoiding a bad-past wallet and picking a good-past one are comparably useful, not one dramatically more reliable than the other.

Q4

Which metric, how consistent, how long?

Which: holding_excess_return — the purest forecasting-skill measure.

How consistent: not very smooth. Among the 252 historically-profitable directional holders the persistence test actually covers, the median wallet earns 54% of profit from a single position; only 44% are profitable in ≥75% of quarters; only ~1% are "steady climbers" (holding ~1% of cohort profit, out of a broader 1,591-wallet population). The edge is real but shows up in rare high-conviction bets. Splitting good-history wallets by concentration shows the low-concentration ones persist forward somewhat more often (49% vs. 44%) — a real but modest extra edge.

How long: the persistence rate fades gradually (the good_past/bad_past gap goes +20pp → +16pp → +13pp from 1 to 3 to 6 months — 66% of the original gap still present at 6 months), with no clean 2–3-month half-life. Re-rank every 1–3 months anyway, since the erosion is real even without a sharp cliff.

Q5

What does this mean for a following strategy?

  1. Rank by forecasting skill (holding-excess-return), restricted to directional holders (trade_holding_rate ≥ 0.5, a count-based check) — not by raw PnL, which rewards bankroll, not skill.
  2. The signal cuts both ways, roughly symmetrically. A good-past wallet is ~10 points more likely than baseline to stay good; a bad-past one is ~5-7 points less likely. Treat avoiding bad wallets and picking good ones as comparably useful.
  3. Re-rank every ~1–3 months — the edge doesn't fully decay within 6 months, but it visibly erodes.
  4. Expect lumpy returns. You'd be copying infrequent, high-conviction bets, not a smooth stream.
  5. Best targets are the ~1% steady climbers — low concentration, profitable median bet.

Following is viable but modest and defensive — not a money printer. Part (b) — which trade to copy — is worth doing, because part (a) cleared the gate: an out-of-sample edge exists.

HONESTY

The caveats, collected

majorCTF / market-maker blind spot

34.0% of volume and 19.6% of resolved PnL is excluded (no cost basis for split/merge-minted tokens). All "trading vs holding" and profitability figures are for the fill-reconstructable set; market-makers are under-measured.

majorResolution date is a proxy

No resolution timestamp exists in the data; we use CLOB end_date (resolution follows within days). Fine for monthly binning, not exact.

moderateThe edge is modest

A ~55-65% conditional persistence rate against a ~50% baseline is a real, statistically significant signal — not a strong one.

moderateActivity-weighted sampling

The non-survivorship cohort over-represents high-frequency wallets (PnL-neutral, but not a uniform sample of all wallets).

minorData window ends 2026-04-28

The legacy subgraph's v2-migration cutoff; the latest forward evaluation windows are shorter.

"No persistence beyond chance" would have been a real, useful result too. It just isn't what we found — there is a modest, honest edge.
