Part 2

From 2.4 million positions to one number per wallet

Part 1 split every position into trading vs holding profit. Part 2 rolls those up to wallets, computes the study's key variable — the trading share of each wallet's profit — and answers the first real question: are there two kinds of players, or one?

01 · WHAT & WHY

The goal

What. Aggregate the position table into per-wallet metrics for every wallet with ≥ 30 resolved positions, then test whether the holder/trader split is bimodal (two real populations) or a continuum.

Which wallets? Not the 278 lum.id top-winners this project started from — that set is winners-only (survivorship-biased). The cohort here is the PnL-neutral pool built in Part 3 by sampling the raw fill tape (activity-weighted, not outcome-weighted): ~6,336 wallets reconstructed, of which the 3,330 with ≥ 30 decomposable resolved positions clear the floor below. The 278 winners are a small subset.

Why. You can only follow a wallet whose edge is in holding — an outcome view you can copy. If a wallet earns by trading the price path, its edge is execution and spread, which you can't replicate. So we need one number per wallet that says where the money came from — and we need to know whether "traders" and "holders" are genuinely different species or just two ends of one scale (the prior clustering suggested a continuum, but on noisy features; this is a clean re-test).

03 · THE KEY VARIABLE

trading_share

Σ trading PnL ÷ Σ |total PnL| = / =

Reading it: this measures where the profit came from, not how much trading happened. ~0 = no trading edge — the profit came from being right about outcomes (a copyable view), or the wallet traded actively but broke even; either way there is nothing to copy in its trading. ~1 = a trader whose profit is execution, not outcomes. Our example sits at essentially zero — it even lost a trivial amount trading, so all its profit came from holding. (A wallet can read ~0 and still trade heavily at break-even — see §07.)

Why divide by Σ|total|, not Σ total? So wins and losses don't cancel in the denominator. A wallet that made $1M trading and lost $1M holding nets ~$0 total — with a plain sum the ratio explodes or misleads. The absolute value asks the honest question: of all the money that moved through this wallet, what fraction came from trading? It's profit-weighted, so it reflects where the dollars actually came from.

05 · THE CENTRAL TEST

Two populations, or one?

The distribution of trading_share across all qualifying wallets. Teal band = holders / no trading edge (<0.2), amber band = traders (>0.8).

Is it one hump or two? Hartigan's dip test — the direct test for modality, and agnostic to the distribution's shape — measures how far the data departs from the best single-humped fit (a bigger dip = more clearly multi-peaked). It returns dip = , p = : the null is "one hump," and a small p would let us reject it — p ≈ 1 is the opposite, no evidence whatsoever against a single peak.

A Gaussian mixture (which describes the data as a blend of Gaussian bumps, with BIC picking how many) did prefer two components — which naively reads as two populations. But look where the two bumps landed: means — sitting almost on top of each other near zero, with no second peak out at the "trader" end. That isn't two clusters; it's one lopsided lump, and the second Gaussian only exists to absorb the skew a single symmetric Gaussian can't bend to fit. That's a known GMM artifact: BIC answers "how many Gaussians fit?", not "how many modes?" — so on this question the dip test is authoritative.

Verdict: one continuous population, overwhelmingly holders. There is no separate "trader" tribe among wallets we can cleanly measure.
